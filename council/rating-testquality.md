FINDINGS:

1. P2 — Declared loopback-only guard does nothing. `tests/test_socks_connect_health.py:17` sets `pytestmark = [pytest.mark.enable_socket, pytest.mark.allow_hosts(["127.0.0.1"])]`. In the installed pytest-socket 0.8.1, `pytest_runtest_setup` (plugin lines 193-199) calls `enable_socket()` and returns early when the `enable_socket` marker is present, so `_resolve_allow_hosts()` never runs and `allow_hosts` is never applied. Verified empirically: with both markers `socket.socket.connect` is the builtin `connect` (all hosts reachable); with `allow_hosts` alone it is `guarded_connect`, and a connect to 192.0.2.1 raises `SocketConnectBlockedError` while a loopback connect succeeds. The FakeSocks fixture binds 127.0.0.1 and the target is the literal "example.com" with no DNS, so nothing escapes today — but the safety claim is unenforced. A future edit (different target, endpoint, or helper) could reach the network and the suite would not stop it. Fix: drop `enable_socket`, keep `allow_hosts(["127.0.0.1"])` alone; add a canary test asserting a non-loopback connect raises `SocketConnectBlockedError`.
